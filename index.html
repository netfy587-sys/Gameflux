The Basics

What Are Video Games?

Video games are interactive digital experiences in which a player makes choices and receives responses from a screen, sound and sometimes physical feedback. They can be played alone, with friends in the same room or with people online across the world.

Games range from short puzzles on a phone to large story adventures that take dozens of hours. They can entertain, teach, challenge and connect people, and today they are one of the most widely enjoyed forms of media.

Interactive

Unlike films, players influence what happens. Decisions and skill shape the outcome.

Creative

Games combine art, music, writing, design and programming into a single experience.

Social

Many games are enjoyed with family and friends, in person or through online communities.

Educational

Puzzle, simulation and strategy games can support problem solving, planning and quick thinking.

Where People Play

Gaming Platforms

Games are available on several kinds of devices. The best choice depends on budget, preferences and who will be playing.

  • Mobile phones and tablets: convenient, widely available and good for short sessions.
  • PCs: flexible hardware, a huge range of titles and strong support for creators.
  • Home consoles: simple setup and a living-room experience, often good for families.
  • Handheld devices: portable gaming with dedicated controls.
  • Cloud and streaming: games run on remote servers and need a stable internet connection.
Always check a game's official age rating and system requirements before downloading or buying.
Behind the Screen

How Video Games Are Made

Making a game is a team effort. Small independent teams may include just a few people, while large studios involve hundreds of specialists. The process usually follows these stages.

Concept

The team defines the idea, audience and core experience.

Design

Rules, levels, characters and goals are planned on paper and in prototypes.

Production

Programmers, artists, writers and sound designers build the game.

Testing

Testers find bugs and check that the game is fun and fair.

Release

The game is published with an age rating and store information.

Updates

Fixes and new content are added based on player feedback.

Careers in Games

The industry includes game designers, programmers, 2D and 3D artists, animators, composers, writers, quality testers, producers and community managers. Many people begin by making small games with free tools and learning from online courses.

Competitive Play

Esports Basics

Esports means organised, competitive video gaming. Players or teams compete in tournaments that may be watched by large online audiences. Like traditional sports, esports has leagues, coaches, rules and training routines.

Teams & Roles

Players often have specific roles and rely on communication and coordination.

Practice

Serious players review matches, practise skills and keep regular schedules including rest.

Fair Play

Tournaments have rules against cheating, harassment and unfair software.

Wellbeing

Breaks, sleep, posture and exercise matter for competitive players as much as for anyone else.

Play Smart

Healthy and Safe Gaming

Healthy Habits

  • Take regular breaks to stretch, look away from the screen and drink water.
  • Keep a consistent sleep routine and avoid late-night marathons.
  • Balance gaming with outdoor activity, hobbies and time with family and friends.
  • Sit with good posture and keep the screen at a comfortable distance.

Online Safety

  • Never share passwords, addresses or other personal details with strangers.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and turn on two-step verification where available.
  • Report and block abusive players using in-game tools.
  • Download games only from official stores to reduce malware risk.

Tips for Parents and Guardians

  • Check the age rating and content descriptors on each game.
  • Use parental controls to manage playtime, purchases and chat settings.
  • Talk openly with children about what they play and who they play with.
  • Be aware of in-app purchases and random reward features, and set spending limits.
  • Play together sometimes – it is a good way to understand what children enjoy.
If gaming begins to interfere with sleep, school, work, health or relationships, talk to a trusted adult, doctor or counsellor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good first game for a beginner?

Easy-to-learn puzzle, casual or adventure games are common starting points. Check the age rating and read reviews from trusted sources.

How long is too long to play?

There is no single answer. What matters is balance: sleep, school or work, physical activity and relationships should not suffer. Children benefit from clear family rules.

Are video games good for learning?

Some games can support problem solving, planning and teamwork, but they should complement, not replace, other learning and activities.

What do age ratings mean?

Ratings such as PEGI or ESRB give guidance about suitable ages and content. They are not a guarantee, so adults should still check a game themselves.
